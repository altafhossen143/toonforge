In the hottest summer anyone could remember, the village well of Fruitpara ran dry, and every tree in the orchard drooped — except one. Old Amm the mango tree still had deep roots, and on her highest branch hung a single golden mango, big and sweet, the last juicy fruit in the village.

Little Koli the koel sang sadly from a bare branch. "If only we had one bite of something juicy, I could sing the rain song my grandmother taught me. But a song needs a happy heart, and everyone's throat is dust."

Greedy Kaw the crow heard this and cackled. He swooped down, snatched the golden mango, and hid it in his nest. "Mine! When the others are desperate enough, they'll trade me all their winter seeds for one fruit!"

But that night, Kaw could not sleep. From below came the sound of the whole village coughing in the dry air — the baby sparrows, old Amm herself, even the proud peacock. And the mango, sitting in his nest, smelled sweeter than anything. One bite would be heaven. Just one.

At dawn, the village woke to a miracle: hanging from Amm's branch by a string was the golden mango, cut into a hundred thin golden slices on a wide banana leaf — enough for every single bird and beast. Nobody saw who did it. Only one black feather lay under the tree.

Koli ate one slice, cleared her throat, and sang the rain song with a full heart. By noon, black clouds rolled over Fruitpara, and the rain came dancing down.

Amm shook her wet leaves and smiled. "A fruit eaten alone is just a fruit," she said. "A fruit shared is a festival." And high above, in a dripping nest, a crow ate the last slice slowly — and for the first time in his life, it tasted sweet.
